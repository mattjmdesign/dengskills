# Design authorship and professional elevation

Use this reference to turn a correct interface into an intentionally composed one. Apply it to visible Build and Improve work, and to audits that judge visual quality.

## 1. Appropriate excellence

Aim for the best design warranted by the product, not the largest visual difference.

- Preserve familiar interaction patterns when they improve recognition, trust, speed, or platform fit.
- Spend expressive risk on one or two high-salience moments that can become specific to the product.
- Treat restraint as a choice, not an absence of design. Quiet work still needs strong proportions, rhythm, typography, and detail.
- Distinguish simplicity from minimalism. Remove what is unnecessary, but keep the content, controls, context, and evidence people need.
- Calibrate character to the audience, task frequency, consequence, category, and environment.
- Do not claim universal taste. When audience evidence is weak, use broadly legible conventions and either a reversible, restrained signature or deliberate restraint; name the assumption.

Visual appeal can influence perceived usability, but it does not prove actual usability. Keep task replay, resilience, and inclusive interaction as independent gates.

## 2. Opportunity triage

Classify each target, not the entire product, as Preserve, Repair, Refine, or Recompose.

### Preserve

Preserve when the surface already has:

- A legible purpose and primary path.
- A page archetype suited to its content.
- Convincing hierarchy, proportions, rhythm, and responsive topology.
- Sound components and interaction conventions.
- Appropriate product character.

Preservation still requires regression verification. Do not restyle a strong surface to make the diff look larger.

Preserve is an affirmative professional judgment, not a default. Support it with rendered evidence against every criterion above. Broad Improve work remains incomplete while a primary surface has a high-confidence, high-leverage Refine or Recompose opportunity. If all primary surfaces genuinely warrant Preserve or Repair, report that evidence plainly rather than presenting the work as a visual redesign.

### Repair

Repair when the composition and product direction are sound but a bounded defect damages the experience:

- A control wraps, clips, overlaps, or loses its label.
- A short-to-long route transition, overlay, font, media, or async state shifts persistent layout unexpectedly.
- Contrast, focus, target sizing, feedback, or recovery fails on a specific component or state.
- A shared primitive creates a repeatable implementation defect without invalidating the page archetype.

Fix the durable root cause and re-render affected siblings. A Repair does not require invented visual alternatives, but it also cannot stand in for broader Refine or Recompose opportunities in the stated scope.

### Refine

Refine when the structure is sound but one or more relationships are weak:

- Scale contrast is timid or noisy.
- Spacing is locally consistent but macro-rhythm is flat.
- Text measure, line breaks, density, or alignment reduces comprehension.
- Controls work but feel visually detached from their context.
- Color, depth, imagery, iconography, or motion lacks a coherent role.
- The product is recognizable only by its logo or accent color.

Refinement can be visibly meaningful without changing the information architecture.

### Recompose

Recompose when relationships, not surface styling, are the problem:

- The primary purpose is not legible at a glance.
- Everything carries similar visual weight.
- Important content is squeezed beside low-value material.
- A generic page scaffold conflicts with the user job or content type.
- Repeated equal sections create monotony and hide priority.
- The responsive model only shrinks or stacks a poor desktop composition.
- A named, high-salience surface has no convincing spatial or product-specific idea.

Do not use personal boredom as evidence. Tie recomposition to comprehension, task flow, audience fit, content expression, brand character, or responsive behavior.

Prioritize with four factors:

    leverage = user impact x visual salience x recurrence x confidence

Fix systemic, high-leverage opportunities before polishing isolated details.

## 3. Direction exploration

For a major surface that needs recomposition or has no clear direction, create two or three distinct hypotheses before implementation. Keep them concise and genuinely different in spatial logic, not merely different accent colors. Represent every candidate at the same viewport with an annotated region map, wireframe, or throwaway render; prose mood, palette, or font changes alone are not composition hypotheses.

Define each hypothesis through:

- Page archetype and focal point.
- Region order, width, proportion, and key alignment lines.
- Density and pacing.
- Type relationships and reading measure.
- Surface, color, imagery, icon, and motion roles.
- Responsive transformation.
- Product-native signature or deliberate-restraint strategy.

Compare candidates against:

1. User purpose and critical content.
2. Immediate orientation and category familiarity.
3. Audience and emotional fit.
4. Product and brand specificity.
5. Responsive and content resilience.
6. Interaction cost and implementation durability.

Choose one and explain why. Do not combine the most expressive feature from every candidate into an incoherent hybrid.

For broad or high-salience work, inspect two to four relevant external references when visual research tools are available. Record one transferable principle and one element not to copy from each. Extract principles, proportions, pacing, and interaction ideas from relevant high-quality products; do not copy their identity, content, or composition wholesale. Prefer references from the same user context or content problem over whatever is currently fashionable.

## 4. Composition and macro-rhythm

Design the page as a sequence of attention, work, and rest.

### Establish a spatial idea

- Create one dominant entry point and a clear supporting flow.
- Use two to four strong vertical key lines across related regions. Let exceptions signal meaning.
- Give important content enough width and negative space to carry its role.
- Use asymmetry when it clarifies priority or creates useful directional flow.
- Balance visual mass; a large quiet field can counter a dense cluster without mirroring it.
- Keep controls and explanations near the decision they support.

### Build rhythm at multiple scales

Micro-rhythm controls local relationships:

    control internals < related items < groups < sections < major transitions

Macro-rhythm shapes the whole page or flow:

- Alternate dense and open passages when task or narrative intensity changes.
- Vary constrained and wide regions when the content changes from reading to comparison, imagery, data, or action.
- Use scale, negative space, imagery, and contrast to create peaks; keep transitional regions quieter.
- Repeat enough structure to create recognition, then vary it when importance or content type changes.
- Avoid a metronomic stack of equal-height sections with identical heading, copy, image, and card patterns.

Spacing tokens create consistency; they do not create rhythm on their own. Judge the sequence of relationships at thumbnail scale and actual size.

### Control complexity

- Keep the category and interaction model recognizable before adding expression.
- Reduce simultaneous focal points, surface treatments, type roles, and shape vocabularies.
- Introduce variety through content-driven composition, not arbitrary component variants.
- Let a signature recur at meaningful moments rather than on every component, or execute deliberate restraint consistently enough to feel authored rather than unfinished.

## 5. Page families and controls

Related pages should share a visual grammar, not necessarily the same layout.

Choose an archetype from the user job and content:

- Editorial or narrative: emphasize sequence, pacing, imagery, and reading measure.
- Directory or catalogue: optimize scanning, comparison, filtering, and continuity into detail.
- Detail or profile: establish identity, essential facts, evidence, and the next meaningful action.
- Research, publication, or documentation: optimize provenance, metadata, long-form reading, and cross-reference.
- Task workspace: prioritize stable placement, density, feedback, keyboard flow, and recovery.
- Dashboard or monitoring: organize recurring decisions and exceptions; do not invent metrics to fill a template.
- Recruiting, contact, or conversion: answer fit, trust, consequence, and next step without generic promotional scaffolds.

For each page family, define what remains shared and what changes because its job changes. A people directory, publication index, research story, news feed, careers page, and contact surface should not become interchangeable collections of equal cards.

Design from content truth:

- Treat the actual populated, sparse, empty, and missing-media states as design inputs, not inconveniences to conceal.
- Never fabricate plausible production people, scholarship, testimonials, activity, metrics, or claims. Use an honest empty state; use clearly isolated fixtures only to test future volume or optional media.
- Do not surface dormant CMS fields, drafts, or personal information without authorization just because they are available to the renderer.
- When media is optional and absent, let text reclaim the space or use an intentional text-led composition. A large generic placeholder usually advertises missing content rather than strengthening the page.
- When media is present, preserve its meaning, crop intent, attribution, loading geometry, and responsive role.

Treat controls as part of the composition:

- Make filters, tabs, search, sorting, and view controls reflect frequency and priority.
- Group labels and controls by the decision they affect.
- Show active state, result impact, and a reset or recovery path.
- Choose wrapping, scrolling, overflow menus, disclosure, or topology changes deliberately.
- Avoid a lonely select plus an unrelated horizontal pill row when a coherent filter model would scan and adapt better.
- Recompose a control group when its structure or visual weight distorts the surrounding content; do not merely round its corners.

Explicitly highlighted controls are named targets. They cannot be counted as addressed when only nearby infrastructure or semantics changed.

## 6. Classical and expressive quality

Evaluate two independent dimensions.

### Classical quality

- Clarity and immediate orientation.
- Order, alignment, proportion, and hierarchy.
- Coherence and consistency.
- Legibility and controlled complexity.
- Precision and completeness.

### Expressive quality

- Product and audience specificity.
- Originality appropriate to the context.
- Emotional tone and memorable character.
- Purposeful variation and sophistication.
- Craft in typography, imagery, materials, motion, and detail.

High classical quality does not automatically create expressive quality. High expression without classical quality often becomes confusing or fragile. Set the target balance in the design thesis:

- High-frequency or high-consequence tools usually favor classical quality with restrained expression.
- Brand-led or narrative surfaces may support greater expression while preserving comprehension.
- Mixed products can place expression in onboarding, storytelling, empty states, or signature workflows while keeping routine work familiar.

Use these four perceptual lenses during critique:

- Simplicity: does the composition feel clear rather than depleted?
- Variety: is there enough meaningful contrast to avoid monotony without losing unity?
- Color quality: is color relational, balanced, and role-driven rather than merely plentiful?
- Craftsmanship: do the details feel deliberate, coherent, and resolved?

These are critique prompts, not a claim that one style will please every audience.

## 7. Rendered critique loop

Source inspection cannot judge composition. Capture the real interface with representative content.

### Pass A: immediate read

View a thumbnail, briefly glance, or apply blur:

- Where does the eye land?
- Are the primary masses and transitions legible?
- Does one region dominate for a reason?
- Is complexity appropriate?
- Does the page have a recognizable spatial idea?

### Pass B: actual-size read

- Can the primary task and content sequence be understood without explanation?
- Do type, measure, alignment, and spacing create a comfortable cadence?
- Are controls proportionate and placed where decisions occur?
- Does each page family express its own job while belonging to the system?
- What still looks assembled rather than composed?

### Pass C: expressive read

- Could this belong to any product after removing the logo?
- Is the product-native signature visible and useful, or is deliberate restraint clearly and consistently executed?
- Does the emotional tone fit the audience and consequence?
- Is the design bland because the thesis is weak, or quiet because restraint is right?
- Is it loud because priority is unclear, or expressive because contrast is purposeful?

### Pass D: comparison and revision

For Improve work, compare baseline and current captures at matching sizes and states. Name:

- What is materially clearer, more coherent, more specific, or more effective.
- What became worse or remains unresolved.
- Which preserved areas were already stronger than the alternatives.

Revise the highest-leverage unresolved issue, render again, and repeat until the mandate is met. One rendered critique-and-revision cycle is required for Create, Recompose, and primary Refine work. A strong first pass still receives a bounded craft revision; Preserve and Repair do not require artificial churn.

## 8. Acceptance

Do not accept a visual improvement merely because the interface is clean, consistent, or technically correct.

A strong result must show:

- Appropriate intervention choices across the stated scope.
- A coherent page archetype and composition.
- Deliberate micro- and macro-rhythm.
- Classical clarity plus the intended level of expressive specificity.
- A design thesis plus either a product-native signature or deliberate restraint visible in the rendered work.
- Familiarity sufficient for operation and distinction sufficient for ownership.
- Stable behavior under real content, states, and responsive transitions.
- Evidence that the result was critiqued, revised, and compared with its baseline or stated intent.

Research behind these lenses includes:

- [Lavie and Tractinsky](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2003.09.002) on classical and expressive aesthetics.
- [Dow and colleagues](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2009/EffectOfParallelPrototyping.pdf) on parallel prototyping.
- [Tuch and colleagues](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/) on visual complexity and prototypicality.
- [Moshagen and Thielsch](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2010.05.006) on perceived visual-aesthetic facets.
- [Reinecke and colleagues](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2013/reinecke13predicting.shtml) on audience variation.
- [Apple's design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles), [Fluent layout guidance](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/layout), and [Carbon's grid guidance](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/2x-grid/usage/) for applied practice.

Neither principles nor citations substitute for testing with the actual audience.
